Median sold prices by postcode district and year, England and Wales

From 1995, one year at a time, from HM Land Registry Price Paid Data.

This page gives the median sold price, and the lower and upper quartile, for each postcode district, each calendar year from 1995 and each of nine splits (all sales, four property types, two tenures, and new-build or existing). Pick an area, a district and a split to see its years, or download the whole area as CSV or JSON. The table is free to use under the Open Government Licence v3.0.

What these figures are, and are not

  • Standard sales only (category A), by transfer date, in nominal pounds. The figures are not adjusted for inflation.
  • The splits are one grouping at a time: all sales, one property type, one tenure, or new-build or existing. They are not combined, so there is no figure for, say, new-build flats.
  • There is a break in late 2013. Before October 2013 every sale was in the standard category. From October 2013 some sales (repossessions, buy-to-lets where identifiable, transfers to companies, and property type “other”) are recorded in a separate category that this table leaves out. Years from 2014 therefore cover a different share of all sales from earlier years. Compare across 2013 with care.
  • This is not an index. The mix of homes sold changes from year to year.
  • Medians and quartiles are left blank, and shown as a dash, where a year has fewer than 5 sales. A year with fewer than 20 sales is marked as a small sample. With exactly 5 sales the lower quartile, median and upper quartile are real sale prices that HM Land Registry already publishes.
  • Districts are those current in the postcode release named below, so sales under postcode districts that have been retired since 1995 are not included.
  • The latest year is as at the month the file was built. Sales registered later are added when the file is next rebuilt, which happens when a new calendar year has ended or the postcode release changes.
  • England and Wales only. Scotland and Northern Ireland are not covered.
