Privacy

What personal data the Yappman Data website collects, why, and your choices.

This notice explains what personal data the Yappman Data website at data.yappman.com collects about visitors, why, and your choices. The grocery data products describe retail prices and hold product and price fields, with no reviewer, seller, account, shopping-basket or delivery-slot fields. Names, brands and promotion text are the retailers’ own text, copied as published, and can include a person’s name. Some of our other products can include the names of people, for example where a register lists a sole trader or a trader list names a business. The section "People whose details appear in public registers" below says how we handle the registers and Trade Info.

Who we are

Yappman Data is a trading name of Abley-Yapp Consulting Limited, registered in England and Wales (company no. 16120444), registered office High Trees Trystings Close, Claygate, Esher, England, KT10 0TF. The controller of personal data on this site is that company. Contact: info@yappman.com.

What we collect, and why

  • Cookies and analytics. This site sets no cookies. We use cookieless analytics (Umami) to count pages and referrers. Like any web host, Umami’s servers receive your IP address and browser details when the page loads its script. Umami records page views, referring sites, browser, operating system, device type and country. We do not send Umami your name or email address. We honour your browser’s Do Not Track setting. Our lawful basis is legitimate interests (understanding how the site is used so we can improve it). See Umami’s privacy information.
  • The announcement bar. If you dismiss it, we remember that for your visit in your browser’s session storage. It never leaves your browser and is cleared when you close the tab.
  • Server logs. The site is hosted on GitHub Pages, which, like any web host, receives your IP address and browser details when you load a page. We do not have access to these logs for our own use. See GitHub’s privacy statement.
  • The Shelf-Price Index page and the home page. They load the published index data directly from Apify’s servers (api.apify.com), so Apify also receives your IP address when you view them. See Apify’s privacy policy.
  • If you email us. We keep your message and email address to reply and to keep a record of the correspondence. Our lawful basis is legitimate interests (answering enquiries and, where relevant, taking steps towards a licence).
  • If we contact an organisation. When we write to an organisation, such as a company, university or public body, about our data, we use a work address the organisation has published itself. We may also write to a named person at a work address their organisation has published, about their role. We do not write to sole traders or partnerships in this way. We hold their name, role, work address and any reply. Our lawful basis is legitimate interests (telling organisations about our data, offering licences, or asking about reuse of theirs). We say where we got the address and how to opt out. A reply of “no” stops our messages, and we keep a short record so they stay stopped. We keep these details for 24 months after our last contact, unless we need them longer for a contract, our accounts or a legal claim, and a short opt-out record for as long as we do outreach. Older copies in our backups and in the version history of our records can last longer; if you ask, we will remove those where we can. To opt out or ask about it, email info@yappman.com.
  • The MCP server. Our MCP server (the address is on the /mcp/ page) runs on Cloudflare Workers. As the host, Cloudflare receives the IP address and request details of anyone who calls it. Since version 0.2.1 the server keeps counts by tool and by the connecting client’s name from a fixed list (anything else is counted as “other”), stamped by Cloudflare with the time of the request; no client version, IP address or request content. A client name is as the client sends it and is not verified. Records written by version 0.2.0 also kept a client version family (major.minor, known clients only); they stay until the three-month retention removes them and are never selected or shown. Cloudflare keeps these records for up to three months, and counts made from them are approximate. The server keeps no record of who called. It has no database of customer data and makes no outbound calls. The only thing it stores is the usage records described above. It answers from data built into it, so nothing you send is passed on to anyone. Cloudflare’s own handling is described in its privacy information.

GitHub (hosting of this site, and private code repositories), Apify (hosting of our tools and of the data they write, including published register rows, and index data on the site), Umami (analytics), Cloudflare (hosting of the MCP server), Zoho (business email hosting) and Google (email for company administration), each acting as a service provider as described above. A provider’s onward transfers to its own subprocessors rely on that provider’s contracts with them, which we have not seen.

People whose details appear in public registers

Some of our data products are built from public registers. Most entries on those registers are organisations. Some are sole traders or small providers whose name is, or contains, a person’s name, and a few could be run from a home. This section is for anyone who may appear in one of the registers listed below.

Our Trade Info product is built from other public sources. It is described under "Other products that can include names" below.

This section describes what runs in our own account and how our tools are designed. Customers who run our tools receive rows in their own accounts and decide for themselves what to do with them.

Which registers. This section covers:

  • the UK Visas and Immigration register of licensed sponsors (workers);
  • the Care Quality Commission (CQC) register of providers and locations in England;
  • the Office for Students (OfS) Register of English higher education providers;
  • the UK public procurement notices used in our Contracts lookup;
  • the Global Legal Entity Identifier (LEI) records used in our LEI lookup;
  • the Commonwealth Register of Institutions and Courses for Overseas Students (CRICOS), the Australian register of education providers that may enrol overseas students;
  • the Gambling Commission’s public register of gambling businesses (Great Britain);
  • the Food Standards Agency’s register of approved establishments for England, Wales and Northern Ireland.

The Contracts, LEI and CRICOS lookups and the CQC care locations feed already exist as products. The sponsor register is used for a change feed released on 3 November 2026. The OfS register is used for a change feed that has not been released yet; we name it here first so you can tell us before it is. The Gambling Commission register is used for a feed released on 5 November 2026. The FSA register of approved establishments is used for a feed that has not been released yet.

We also keep copies of some other public registers for work that has not been released. You can ask about them by the route below.

Where it comes from. We take the data from each register’s publisher, from the file or interface the publisher provides. We do not collect it from you.

  • Sponsor register: the file published by UK Visas and Immigration.
  • CQC: the care directory file of locations CQC regulates, which CQC publishes without a key, usually weekly, under the Open Government Licence v3.0. We also keep, for research only, a copy of records from the CQC public interface; it feeds no product.
  • OfS Register: the Register as published by the Office for Students.
  • Contracts: the notices published on Find a Tender, Contracts Finder and Public Contracts Scotland.
  • LEI: the records published through the Global Legal Entity Identifier Foundation (GLEIF).
  • CRICOS: the Institutions file and the Courses file of the register, published on data.gov.au by the Australian Government Department of Education under the Creative Commons Attribution 2.5 Australia licence.

What each product holds.

  • Sponsor register. We download the register file and compare each file with the previous one to record what changed: an organisation added, removed, or moved to a different route, type or rating. A row is a change: organisation name, town, county, route, old and new type and rating, dates and the name of the source file. The published register does not show a dated record of its own changes, so we build one. We also plan to publish a monthly net count of additions and removals, rounded to the nearest 5 and left blank when a month’s count is under 20.
  • CQC. A row is one location shown, or one change between two CQC files: the location name, when both it and its provider’s name pass our organisation rule; the outward part of the postcode; local authority; region; service types and specialisms; the CQC location id and provider id; and the dates of the CQC file and of our reading. A change record says a location was listed, is no longer listed, or that its details changed. Rows do not return the provider’s name, address lines, phone numbers, websites, coordinates, ratings, or the names of registered managers or nominated individuals. A location whose name, or whose provider’s name, does not clearly look like an organisation’s is left out whole and counted (explained below). Separately, the records we keep on our own computer for research, fetched from CQC’s public interface, hold the full location record as the CQC interface returns it, which includes the name, address, phone number, website, coordinates and ratings, with registered-manager contact details removed. Those records feed no product.
  • OfS Register. A row is a change to the Register: the provider’s legal name, trading name, UKPRN (its reference number), registration category, fee-limit class, the status of its teaching-excellence entry, degree-awarding powers, university title, the conditions recorded against it, old and new values, the dates of changes and the source sheet. Conditions and old and new values can be free text copied as published. Rows are designed not to return the Register’s contact address, email address and website columns.
  • Contracts. A row holds the notice title, the buyer (an organisation), value, currency, CPV codes, regions, stage, dates, a link to the notice and notice ids. It has no supplier, winner, contact, address or description field. A title is free text copied as published. Email addresses, UK phone numbers and a name written straight after cue words such as "contact", "FAO" or "write to" are replaced with [redacted] by pattern, so a name written another way, including a supplier named in a title, can remain.
  • LEI. A row holds the LEI, legal name, other names, category, legal form, jurisdiction, entity and registration status, dates, registration number, and the legal and headquarters address down to city only. For a recognised sole proprietor, or an arrangement named after a person (a settlement, a family or will trust, "estate of"), the names, registration number and addresses are left blank and listed as left out. In name and status searches those entities are left out of the results and counted.
  • CRICOS. A row is one education provider: the provider code; the provider’s name, when our name rule shows it, with a flag saying whether the name is left out; the institution type; the state, postcode and city of its postal address; its institution capacity (a number); its number of courses and of courses the register marks as not expired; its website address, when neither its trading name nor its institution name is left out; and the date of the data. Rows do not return postal address lines, location names, contact details or course fees. The file we download includes providers’ postal addresses and websites; we read it in memory for the run and do not return, keep or use the address lines. A provider that is a sole trader can appear through its name, if our rule shows it, and through its postcode and city, which can be a home locality. The lookup keeps nothing between runs, apart from the exceptions in the table below.

The files we keep from the publishers hold more than our rows return. The sponsor file has the same columns our rows use, but for every entry, including the names we leave out of what we publish. The OfS Register file can hold contact columns that our rows do not return. The CQC research records, as described above, hold the full location record as the CQC interface returns it, which includes the name, address, phone number, website, coordinates and ratings, with registered-manager contact details removed; the CQC file we download for the feed is read in memory and deleted within 14 days. We keep these files on our own computer. How long is in the table below. The CRICOS lookup reads the register files in memory and keeps no copy. Our test files hold a small set of register rows (see the table below). Until 29 October 2026 we also kept a local copy of the CRICOS register files for a planned record of changes; it has been deleted.

Names that look like a person are left out and counted. For the sponsor register and the CQC feed, and for the OfS register (not released yet), we use a rule that is designed to tell whether a name looks like an organisation. If a name looks like a sole trader or a person (for example a name with no company-type ending, unless it carries a school or education word; a title such as Mr or Dr; "care of"; or "trading as" a person), the row is designed to be left out of what we publish, and only a count of the rows left out is kept in what we publish. The source files we keep still hold those names. A name let through because it carries a school or education word can be a person’s name. For CQC the whole location is left out, as described above, unless both the location name and its provider’s name pass the rule. For LEI the same idea applies, and already runs, for the sole proprietors and person-named arrangements described above.

The rule is a pattern test. It can leave out a genuine organisation. It can let through a limited company named after a person (for example "Example Surname Care Services Limited"; for the sponsor register, a name made of initials, a surname and a company-type ending, such as "J Smith Ltd", is designed to be left out), a company trading under a person’s name that the rule does not recognise, and a school, academy or tuition business named after one person, because these appear on the register as published there. The LEI test can miss a person. We are still measuring how well the sponsor and OfS tests work. For CQC, see the measurement in “CQC England Care Locations” below.

The CRICOS name rule. For the CRICOS lookup the rule works from the other direction. For each provider we use the trading name if the rule shows it, else the institution name if it does, else no name. The rule leaves a name out unless it finds a sign that the name belongs to an organisation. It is a pattern test. It can leave out a genuine organisation (for example a name of one core word followed by “Training Pty Ltd”), and it can let through a college named after a person. In our measurement of 28 October 2026, names were left out for about 24% of providers (365 of 1,547). A name made of what looks like a person’s name and a word such as College, with no company ending, is left out even when it belongs to an organisation. A search by name matches the names we show, not the names we leave out.

Free text and links. A Contracts title, and the conditions and old and new values in an OfS row, are free text copied as published, and can mention a person. The notice link in a Contracts row and the record link in an LEI row open the publisher’s own page, which we do not filter. For a person-named LEI record, the source page may show details our row leaves out.

What we do it for. The sponsor feed is designed to record dated changes to the sponsor register, which the published register does not show. The CQC feed records which locations are listed in CQC’s directory file and what changed between its files. The OfS feed is designed to record dated changes to its register. The Contracts lookup lets a buyer or bidder search public notices. The LEI lookup lets a user look up and check entities by name, status or LEI. The CRICOS lookup lets a user look up which providers are on the register, by state, type, postcode or name.

The sponsor register change feed

This part is for anyone who may appear in the UK Visas and Immigration register of licensed sponsors (workers). The feed was released on 3 November 2026.

Who is responsible. Yappman Data (see “Who we are” above) decides how the feed works and is responsible for it. Contact: info@yappman.com.

What we hold about you. If your organisation is on the register, the change records are designed to hold its name, town, county, route, type and rating, the kind of change and its dates. An organisation’s name can be a sole trader’s name, or the name of a company or school named after a person: see “What each product holds” and “Names that look like a person are left out and counted” above.

What it is for. The feed is designed to record, by date, which organisations were added to, removed from or changed on the register, which the published register does not show, for people who follow sponsor licence changes, such as recruiters, immigration advisers, researchers and journalists. It is designed to say only what the register shows, for example “no longer listed”, and nothing about why a change happened.

Lawful basis. Our lawful basis is legitimate interests. We set out our reasons in a written assessment, which we signed on 27 October 2026. The interests are ours (a data product), our customers’ (following changes to a register) and the public’s (transparency about a statutory register).

Your right to object. You can object at any time to our use of your details for this feed. Use the route in “Asking us to see, correct or remove an entry” below; we will reply within one month.

Your other rights. You can ask to see what we hold about you, to have it corrected or removed, and to have us restrict our use of it while we look into your request. Use the same route; we will consider each request and reply within one month.

Where the details come from. From the register file that UK Visas and Immigration publishes on GOV.UK (Register of licensed sponsors: workers). It contains public sector information licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0. We are not affiliated with or endorsed by the Home Office or UK Visas and Immigration. We do not collect your details from you.

Who receives them. Apify, our hosting platform, holds the change records we publish and runs the tool, and customers who run the tool receive the records in their own Apify accounts. The AI assistant we use has read register files while we built and checked our tools (see “AI model sessions” below), and the device-management service described in “Where the processing happens” has technical access to the computer we use. See also “Who receives data” above.

Transfers outside the UK. Our service providers (Apify, which runs our tools and stores the datasets they write; Zoho, which hosts our business email; Google, which provides email for company administration; and GitHub, which holds our private code) and the AI assistant provider may process data outside the UK. Apify is based in the Czech Republic and may transfer data to the United States and other countries where it or its subprocessors operate. The EU standard contractual clauses (Module Four) with the UK International Data Transfer Addendum cover the transfer from Apify back to us; Apify’s transfers to its own subprocessors rely on Apify’s own contracts with them (Apify’s data processing agreement of 8 July 2026). We have not put a transfer safeguard in place for the AI assistant provider.

How long we keep them. We keep the two newest register files, and any file not yet compared with the one before it, and delete older files. We applied this rule for the first time on 2 November 2026, and we apply it every week. Change records are kept for 24 months, on our computer and in the store on Apify; none is yet older than that. If more than 3% of the register changes in one day, we hold that day’s changes back for checking, for up to 14 days while we decide, and then for 30 days after the hold ends (because we release or discard the changes, a newer file supersedes them, or the 14 days run out). Our computer also held copies made when we installed our tools on 27 October 2026, and older state files from 17 October 2026, which the rule above does not cover. We deleted the state files and the install rehearsal copy on 3 November 2026. The install backup is kept for about 14 days after the install, then deleted. If the feed stops, we delete what we hold for it, including the published change records, within 30 days; a few technical copies, such as a Windows restore point, an install backup kept for about 14 days after an install, or ids of renamed entries that are not names, may last a little longer, and our reply to you will say if one applies. Copies that customers already hold are outside our control.

What a removal does. We keep a removal list of organisation names in a private folder on our computer. The tool that makes the change records applies the list each time it runs, so a listed entry is left out of later publishing. A second tool rewrites the stored change history so that it no longer holds a listed entry; we run it by hand, and it has not yet been used on a real request. When you ask us to restrict an entry, we add the name to the list on receipt while we consider your request. While an entry is restricted, we keep collecting and comparing it so the history stays correct, but it is not published. If we lift a restriction, we will tell you why in writing first. If you ask us to remove an entry, or object, we add the name to the same list and rewrite the stored change history for that entry; the published records are rebuilt from that history at the next publish. The register files we download from GOV.UK still hold the entry for as long as the published register does; our reply will say so. A record may also be missing because of our name rule, and the feed does not say why a record is missing. A removal cannot take back records a customer has already received, copies that others have made, or the register itself. To ask for one, use the route in “Asking us to see, correct or remove an entry” below.

Complaints. You can complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office at any time; see “If you are unhappy” below.

CQC England Care Locations

This part is for anyone who may appear in the Care Quality Commission (CQC) directory of care locations in England, the file CQC publishes of the locations it regulates. The feed was released on 3 November 2026.

Who is responsible. Yappman Data (see “Who we are” above) decides how the feed works and is responsible for it. Contact: info@yappman.com.

Where the details come from. From the care directory file that CQC publishes without a key, usually weekly, under the Open Government Licence v3.0. The Open Government Licence does not cover personal data. We download the file by hand and read it in memory; we do not collect the details from you. We are not affiliated with or endorsed by CQC.

What a row holds. One row per location shown, or per change: the location name, when both it and its provider’s name pass our organisation rule; the outward part of its postcode only (for example SW11); its local authority and region; its service types and specialisms; its CQC location id and CQC provider id; the date of the CQC file and the date we read it. A change record says that a location was listed, is no longer listed, or that its details changed between two CQC files, dated by CQC’s file date. “No longer listed” means the location was not in the later file; it does not mean the service has closed, and the feed never says why a change happened.

What is left out. The provider’s name is read for the rule only and is not returned. The file’s address lines, phone number, website, “also known as”, location web address and date of latest check are never extracted, stored or returned; a damaged row is scanned in memory for id-shaped text only. CQC’s files of registered managers and nominated individuals are never downloaded. The feed returns no ratings, no coordinates and no free text other than the location name. Full postcodes are not returned.

Where an individual can appear. Most locations are run by companies, charities and public bodies. A location or provider whose name is, or hides, a person’s name (a sole trader, a small provider, a service run from a home) is what our rule is for: unless both the location name and the provider name clearly look like an organisation’s, the location is left out whole, with no row, no change record, no id and no replacement code; only a count of locations left out is kept. On the CQC file of 22 October 2026 the rule showed 9,787 of 57,107 locations (about 17%) and left out 47,320. An organisation-named location in a small area can still point to the household that runs it, through its outward postcode, local authority, service type and CQC id.

The name rule. It is a pattern test, not a judgement about any person. It can leave out a genuine organisation, and it can let through a company named after a person, because that is how the name appears on the register. On 30 October 2026 we checked 300 of the roughly 9,800 location names the rule shows, drawn from the CQC file of 28 October 2026: none looked like a person’s name and none was unsure. The sample’s labels were deleted the same day.

What it is for. A searchable, dated record of what is listed in CQC’s England directory and what changed between its files, for people who follow the care sector, such as investors, consultants, local authorities and recruiters, where CQC publishes only the current file. It says only that CQC’s published file changed, never why, and nothing about the quality or safety of any service.

Lawful basis. Our lawful basis is legitimate interests. We set out our reasons in a written assessment, which we signed on 30 October 2026. The interests are ours (a data product), our customers’ (following changes to a register) and the public’s (transparency about regulated care).

We do not write to providers individually. The file we download carries address and phone columns, which we never extract or keep, and we hold no other contact details for the people behind an entry. Collecting them to send a notice would be a new use of them, so we publish this information here instead.

Your right to object. You can object at any time to our use of your details for this feed. Email info@yappman.com with the CQC location id or provider id; if you do not have it, give the name as it appears on CQC’s register. Use the route in “Asking us to see, correct or remove an entry” below; we will reply within one month. You can also ask to see what we hold about you, to have it corrected or removed, and to have us restrict our use of it, by the same route.

What a removal does. If you ask us to remove an entry, or object, we add the CQC location id or provider id to a removal list held on our computer, outside our code. The list holds ids, the kind of id and the date, not names. A listed location is treated as left out: it is not shown, no change record is made for it, and our removal tool rewrites the stored current records and change records without it. A location can also be missing because of our name rule, and the feed does not say why a location is missing. A removal cannot take back rows a customer has already received, copies that others have made, or CQC’s own register; the file we download from CQC still holds the entry for as long as CQC publishes it, and we delete each file within 14 days. We do not edit CQC’s record; corrections go to CQC.

Who receives them. Apify, our hosting platform, holds the published records in a restricted store in our account and runs the tool, and customers who run the tool receive rows in their own Apify accounts. The device-management service described in “Where the processing happens” has technical access to the computer we use. See also “Who receives data” above.

Transfers outside the UK. Apify is based in the Czech Republic and may transfer data to the United States and other countries where it or its subprocessors operate. The EU standard contractual clauses (Module Four) with the UK International Data Transfer Addendum cover the transfer from Apify back to us; Apify’s transfers to its own subprocessors rely on Apify’s own contracts with them (Apify’s data processing agreement of 8 July 2026). Our other service providers are listed in “Who receives data” above.

How long we keep them. On Apify we keep the current set of records and the one before it, and the change records from the first CQC file we stored onwards. The change records are kept until a deletion job exists: we have decided a deletion rule and built a tool to apply it, but it has not run yet, and we will update this page when it has. The CQC file we download is deleted by hand once the week’s publishing is checked, within 14 days. Separately from this feed, we keep a research copy of records from CQC’s public interface on our computer; it feeds no product and is described under “What each product holds” and in the table below. If a week’s file looks wrong to our checks (for example a large drop in locations or many more changes than usual), we publish nothing from it. Rows a customer has received sit in that customer’s own account, outside our control.

Complaints. You can complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office at any time; see “If you are unhappy” below.

Gambling Commission register of gambling businesses

This part is for anyone who may appear in the Gambling Commission’s public register of gambling businesses, which covers Great Britain. The feed was released on 5 November 2026.

Who is responsible. Yappman Data (see “Who we are” above) decides how the feed works and is responsible for it. Contact: info@yappman.com.

Where it comes from. The Gambling Commission’s public register of gambling businesses, which covers Great Britain. We read four of its files (businesses, licences, trading names, domain names) once a day. We do not collect the data from you. The Commission publishes this register on data.gov.uk under the Open Government Licence v3.0, and gave written consent on 19 October 2026 to our use of it for the purposes we described to it. We are not affiliated with the Gambling Commission.

What we hold about organisations. For each organisation: account number, licence account name, licence number, status, type, activity, start and end dates, trading names and website domains, as the register publishes them. Each row is dated with the day the file was published.

Sole traders. Some accounts are held by sole traders, whose licence account name is a person’s name. We leave out every account that our name rule cannot show to be an organisation: about a third of accounts, many of them genuine firms. For those accounts we keep only the account number, on our own computer, so the account stays left out if its name later changes, and counts of their licence rows (a count under 5 is stored as “<5”). Their names, licences, trading names and domains are not stored.

Names inside organisations’ entries. A founder’s or director’s name can appear inside an organisation’s trading name or domain. We keep those entries because the Commission publishes them for checking a business, they describe the business, and you can ask us to remove one (below). The rule is a pattern test. It can leave out a genuine organisation and it can let through a sole trader who trades under a company-style name.

Why we use it. We rely on legitimate interests. The interests are ours (a data product); our customers’ (checking whether a business, licence, trading name or domain is on the register, and what changed: for example payment providers, advertising networks, compliance teams, researchers and journalists); and public transparency about who is licensed to offer gambling. We state only what the register shows and mark each row with a notice to check the Commission’s register.

Who receives it.

  • Customers of our paid Apify Actor receive only rows for accounts our rule treats as organisations, in their own Apify accounts.
  • Apify runs our tools and holds the store the Actor reads, as our processor.
  • An AI assistant provider, used under consumer terms, not as our processor, has been used in a session on the same computer for this work. We work from counts and invented examples and do not open the stored files.
  • GitHub holds our private code.
  • For who can technically reach the computer we use, see “Where the processing happens” below.

Outside the UK. Our service providers (Apify, an AI assistant provider and GitHub) may process data outside the UK. See “Who receives data” above, or ask us.

How long we keep it.

  • Extracts and change records on our computer: 12 months. The tool that deletes older ones is not built yet; the first deletion falls due in October 2027.
  • The Apify store: the latest extract and at most 12 months of released changes, each record rewritten at every update.
  • The set of sole traders’ account numbers and the counts: while the service runs.
  • When we stop the service, we delete the Apify store and every record in it, and our local data folder, including that set and those counts.
  • Customers’ own run datasets are theirs; see below.

The CRICOS register lookup (Australia)

This part is for anyone who may appear in the Commonwealth Register of Institutions and Courses for Overseas Students (CRICOS), the Australian register of education providers that may enrol overseas students. The lookup was released on 29 October 2026.

Who is responsible. Yappman Data (see “Who we are” above) decides how the lookup works and is responsible for it. Contact: info@yappman.com.

Where the details come from. From the Institutions file and the Courses file of the register, which the Australian Government Department of Education publishes on data.gov.au under the Creative Commons Attribution 2.5 Australia licence. Each run reads both files. We do not collect the details from you. We are not affiliated with or endorsed by the Department.

What a row holds. One row per provider: the provider code; the provider’s name, when our name rule shows it; a flag saying whether the name is left out; the institution type; the state, postcode and city of its postal address; its institution capacity (a number); its number of courses and of courses the register marks as not expired; its website address, when neither its trading name nor its institution name is left out; and the date of the data.

What is left out. Postal address lines, location names, contact details and course fees are not returned. The Institutions file we download includes providers’ postal addresses and websites. We read it in memory for the run, and we do not return, keep or use the address lines. A website address can contain a name, so it is left out whenever either name is left out.

Where an individual can appear. Most providers are companies, universities and TAFEs. A provider that is a sole trader can appear through its name, if our rule shows it, and through its postcode and city. A row whose name is left out still gives the provider code and the postal state, postcode and city, which for a sole trader can be a home locality.

The name rule. See “The CRICOS name rule” above for how it works, its measured share of names left out and what it can get wrong. It is a pattern test, not a judgement about any person.

What it is for. A dated lookup of which providers are on the register, by state, type, postcode or name, for education agents, researchers and businesses. The rows are a dated copy of the register’s files. A provider that is missing from a result has not necessarily closed.

Lawful basis. Our lawful basis is legitimate interests. We set out our reasons in a written assessment, which we signed on 29 October 2026. The interests are ours (a data product) and our customers’ (looking up and searching providers).

We do not contact providers individually. The file we download includes providers’ postal addresses and websites. We do not use them to contact anyone. Writing to each provider would be a new use of them, so we publish this information here instead.

Your right to object. You can object at any time to our use of your details for this lookup. Email info@yappman.com with the provider code; if you do not have it, give the name as it appears on the register. Use the route in “Asking us to see, correct or remove an entry” below; we will reply within one month. You can also ask to see what we hold about you, to have it corrected or removed, and to have us restrict our use of it, by the same route.

What a removal does. If you ask us to remove an entry, or object, we add your provider code to a removal list held in the tool’s configuration. The list holds provider codes, not names. A code is added by a reviewed change and takes effect with the next build; from then the provider is not returned by the lookup. A removal cannot take back rows a customer has already received, copies that others have made, or the Department’s own record. We do not edit the Department’s record; corrections go to the Department.

Who receives them, and sending data overseas. Apify, our hosting platform, runs the lookup and holds the result datasets. Customers who run it receive the rows in their own Apify accounts, which can be in any country. Apify, which processes this data for us, is based in the Czech Republic and may process it in the United States and other countries where it or its subprocessors operate. The EU standard contractual clauses (Module Four) with the UK International Data Transfer Addendum cover the transfer from Apify back to us; Apify’s transfers to its own subprocessors rely on Apify’s own contracts with them. The register data is already published by the Australian Government. This is our statement under Australian Privacy Principle 8. The AI assistant we use has read the CRICOS test files and test output while we built and checked the lookup (see “AI model sessions” below), and the device-management service described in “Where the processing happens” has technical access to the computer we use. See also “Who receives data” above.

How long we keep them. The lookup keeps nothing between runs: no copy of the register, no result and no history. The exceptions are our own private test runs and our test files; the sample we used to check the name rule was deleted on 29 October 2026; see the table below. Until 29 October 2026 we also kept a local copy of the register files for a planned record of changes; it has been deleted. Rows sit in the account of the customer who ran the lookup, under that account’s own retention settings, and we cannot recall them.

Other products that can include names

Trade Info (UK importers and exporters). It reads the monthly lists of UK importers and exporters that HMRC publishes (HMRC UK Trade Info: bulk files for the latest month, and HMRC’s interface for older months and lookups). HMRC lists sole traders’ names next to companies’ names.

  • Returned: the trader’s name as published; the outward part of its postcode only (for example EX2); the flow (import or export); the month; commodity codes; the source and its date; HMRC’s trader id where HMRC’s interface gives one; how the row matched your search; traderKey, computed from the name and outward code; and, in alert mode, alertFingerprint (a fingerprint of the organisation returned, described under Alerts).
  • Not returned: the full postcode, contact details, and trade values, volumes, customers or suppliers. There is no address field; address columns in HMRC’s files are read past and not kept.
  • Left out and counted: a name is returned when it carries an organisation marker such as Ltd or PLC. Names without one, names with a title, and names with a trading-as, care-of or address part are left out and counted.
  • Known misses: a company named after a person is returned as published. The rule can miss some people, for example a name written with spaced initials such as "P L C" or "L T D", and it can leave out a genuine organisation.
  • Alerts: the alert mode records unsalted fingerprints of returned organisations only, not names, in a store in the account of the person who runs it. A fingerprint can be reversed by guessing.
  • Asking us: use the route in "Asking us to see, correct or remove an entry" below. We will reply within one month.

AusTender Contract Notices. This Apify Actor reads Australian Government contract notices from the AusTender API, published by the Department of Finance under CC BY 3.0 AU. A notice can name a supplier who is a person, such as a sole trader. The Actor outputs no supplier names, ABNs or other supplier identifiers, and no contact details, addresses or notice descriptions. Each row carries the notice’s CN id and a link to the notice’s record on the AusTender API, which names the supplier. To have a notice left out, email info@yappman.com with its CN id. To object to your notices, including future ones, also give your ABN: from the next build after we update our list, the Actor leaves out any notice it reads that carries that ABN. We act within one month. Our list holds an ABN only as a keyed hash; the Actor reads ABNs from AusTender in memory to check them against the list and never stores or outputs them. We keep your request email for as long as your objection stands, an exception to our 24-month email rule below.
