What this measures, and what it doesn't

It measures posted online prices. Each week the online catalogue of four UK supermarkets — Tesco, Sainsbury's, Asda and Aldi — is read from its website. Products present in both this week and last week are matched by the retailer's own product id, and the index is the geometric mean (a Jevons index) of their price ratios, with equal weight to each product within a retailer-class cell, equal weight to each retailer, no spending weights, chained week on week from a base of 100 at ISO week 2026-W39 (the closed series, weeks 39 and 40) and, after the skipped week 41, from a new base of 100 at ISO week 2026-W42. Four-week changes are empty across the gap (see the notice). Shelf price only: loyalty-card prices and multibuy promotions are recorded separately and excluded here.

It is not CPI, and it is not a measure of prices paid. The ONS consumer prices index uses scanner data with transaction quantities and includes discounts actually taken; this index has no quantities and no transactions. Treat it as a fast, product-level indicator of what the four retailers are asking online, published days after the week it describes.

Known limits. Online catalogues only, so in-store-only lines are absent; no store is chosen for Tesco and Sainsbury's, and one fixed store or service point is used for Asda and Aldi, so prices may differ in other stores, formats, regions and channels; Aldi publishes no barcodes, so cross-retailer joins there use name and pack size. Category rows can rest on fewer than four retailers where one retailer's cell is too thin. The four-week figure is empty until four weeks exist. There is no year-on-year figure until a year exists. A product whose pack size is missing in one of the two weeks, and whose price per unit cannot be compared, is compared on its shelf price, so a smaller pack at the same price that week is missed. Retailers round the price per unit they show, so a comparison on it carries a small error either way. Suspect moves (a price that rises by 200% or more, or falls to a third or less, in one week) are excluded and listed in the weekly note.

Coverage. About 73,000 products a week in the weekly snapshots (73,745 in the week 40 snapshot of 4 October 2026); matched counts are shown on every row. A week is published only if all four retailers contribute to the headline; a retailer whose snapshot covers far fewer products than the week before is named in the metadata (coverageWarnings). Weeks are never backfilled or interpolated. Morrisons joins from November 2026, and the index continues on the four-retailer basis with Morrisons reported separately until a full year of five-retailer data exists.

Methodology and due diligence for the underlying data: data.yappman.com. Weekly "what moved" notes: UK Grocery Price Moves. The product-level data behind the index is licensed separately.
