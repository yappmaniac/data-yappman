The ABS figures shown are the original series, not seasonally adjusted. A one-month change in an original series includes the usual seasonal movement for that month.

Why they are not comparable

  • Different periods. The latest CPI figures are for September 2026. Of our UK index, only week 39 of the closed series (data from 27 September 2026) falls in September; the later weeks are in October and November, and the current series starts at week 42 (base 100). The AU index starts in October 2026 (week 41, base 100). A CPI annual rate compares a month with the same month a year earlier; our indices have no year-on-year figure until a year exists.
  • Different things measured. Our indices follow the posted online shelf prices of the same products at Tesco, Sainsbury’s, Asda and Aldi (UK) and Coles, Woolworths and Aldi Australia (AU): shelf price only, with loyalty-card prices and multibuy promotions left out, no quantities and no transactions. The CPI is a measure of consumer price inflation built by each statistics office from its own sources and weights (how our index is built).
  • Different weighting. Our indices give equal weight to each product within a retailer-category cell and to each retailer, with no spending weights.
  • Different scope. Our UK headline covers all categories at four retailers; the food and soft drinks subtotal is the nearest to the CPI food group, but it is not defined the same way. Our AU headline covers the categories in its like-for-like set, not food alone, and Coles is a partial slice of its range.

Our indices are shelf prices only, not CPI and not a measure of prices paid; they are released under CC BY 4.0.
