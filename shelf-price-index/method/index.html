UK Online Shelf-Price Index: how it is measured

The method behind the UK Online Shelf-Price Index. Field definitions are in the data dictionary.

What it measures

The index follows the posted online shelf prices of the same products at Tesco, Sainsbury's, Asda and Aldi from one week to the next. It is not what shoppers paid, it is not the ONS Consumer Prices Index (CPI), and it is not a forecast. Offers that need a loyalty card, and multibuy promotions, are left out of the published figures.

Method

Each week the online catalogue of each retailer is read from its website, and each week's snapshot is compared with the week before. A product is matched by the retailer's own product id, and only products present in both weeks with a price in both are used.

For each retailer and each broad product class, the weekly change is the geometric mean (a Jevons index) of the products' price ratios: equal weight to each product within a retailer-class cell, equal weight to each retailer, no spending weights.

  • Suspect moves. A price that multiplies by 3 or more in one week (a rise of 200% or more), or falls to a third or less (a fall of 66.7% or more), is treated as a probable pricing error, or the correction of one, and left out of the index. It is listed in the weekly report.
  • Pack-size changes. When a product's pack size changes between the two weeks, the change in its price per unit is used; from 2026-W44 large moves of that kind are counted and reported, but are not yet excluded by the check for probable pricing errors. If the price per unit cannot be worked out, the product is left out that week. A pack size missing in one of the two weeks is treated as unknown, not as a change (from 2026-W43). From 2026-W44, in that case the price per unit is compared when both weeks give one in the same measure, and it goes through the same check for probable pricing errors as the shelf price; otherwise the shelf price is compared, as it is when neither week gives a pack size. 2026-W43 was published under the first rule alone.
  • Thin cells. A cell (one retailer and class) with fewer than 20 used products that week (matched products less those left out as suspect moves or for a pack-size change) keeps last week's level and is marked as not updated (status carried, or insufficient when none is used).
  • Classes. There are 16 classes, assigned by keyword rules on the retailer's category names and product names, so they are approximate. A class row can rest on fewer than four retailers: if a retailer has too few used products in a class that week, its cell is left out of that class's figure, and the row's matched count and status show it.
  • The headline always includes all four retailers, and a week is not published unless it does.

Base weeks and the restart

The weekly changes are chained from a base of 100. The first series has its base at ISO week 2026-W39 and covers weeks 39 and 40. The collection of week 41 (11 October 2026) did not complete, so week 41 is skipped and the index restarted with a new base of 100 at 2026-W42; the first weekly change of the new series is for week 43 (notice). The earlier levels stay in the dataset as a closed series, there is no level for week 41, and four-week changes are empty across the gap. In the new series the four-week change stays empty until 2026-W46. Each vintage's meta item names its base.

There is no year-on-year figure, because the series starts in 2026.

Coverage and limits

  • Over 75,000 products a week in the weekly snapshots (75,835 in the week 43 snapshot of 25 October 2026). Matched counts are shown on every row.
  • Only what the retailers show online is covered: no in-store or regional prices.
  • A product relaunched under a new identifier drops out of the match, so its price change is missed. A product whose pack size is missing in one of the two weeks, and whose price per unit cannot be compared, is compared on its shelf price, so a smaller pack at the same price that week is missed. Retailers round the price per unit they show, so a comparison on it carries a small error either way.
  • When a retailer's snapshot covers far fewer products than the week before, the meta names it (coverageWarnings); the week is still published only if all four retailers contribute to the headline. Weeks are never backfilled or interpolated.
  • Barcode coverage, completeness and run success for the weekly snapshots behind the index are on the data quality page; how the snapshots are collected is in the methodology, and a short recording of the scraper is on the demos page.
  • Planned: Morrisons will be reported separately once its weekly snapshots and series are in place. The index continues on the four-retailer basis until a full year of five-retailer data exists.
  • Product-level shelf prices from Tesco, Sainsbury’s, Asda and Aldi can be read on demand with the UK Supermarket Price Scraper, a pay-per-event Actor on the Apify Store.

Release and cadence

Intended weekly, normally on Monday, when the week's snapshots pass the method's checks. The first vintage was published on Monday 5 October 2026 (the 2026-W40 figures, with 2026-W39 as the base). Each week is added to the public dataset uk-shelf-price-index as a vintage. Published index values are not revised except to correct an error. A correction is published as a new, clearly marked revision. The licence is CC BY 4.0.

Download the full series: CSV or JSON. Only aggregates are published: no product names, ids, URLs or prices.

Methodology note for 2026-W43

New series from 2026-W42. No index was produced for 2026-W41, so the chain cannot run from 2026-W40 into 2026-W42. A new series starts with 2026-W42 = 100. The first series (base 2026-W39 = 100, covering 2026-W39 and 2026-W40) is left as published and is not joined to the new one. Levels in the two series cannot be compared.

Missing pack sizes at Aldi. In the 2026-W42 Aldi snapshot, 1,263 products that were in both weeks had no pack size, and none of them had a unit price. In 2026-W43 all 1,263 have a pack size and 1,250 have a unit price. The cause is not known: the collection run finished normally and recorded nothing about these fields, so it cannot show whether Aldi sent no value or a value in a form the collector did not read. The pack sizes in the Aldi snapshots changed form between 2026-W40 (mostly in kilograms with decimals, such as 0.48 kg) and 2026-W43 (mostly in grams, such as 475 g), while the way the collector reads them did not change, and the gap falls in that change. It affects 13 of the 14 top-level Aldi departments in the snapshot, not one. The shelf price comes from the same field in both weeks, and the share of these products whose shelf price changed between 2026-W42 and 2026-W43 (2.4%) is close to that of other Aldi products (2.6%).

A pack size missing in one of the two weeks is treated as unknown, not as a change, so these products are compared on their shelf price. Of the 1,032 that had a pack size in 2026-W40, 1,008 had the same pack quantity in 2026-W43 (214 of them differ only by rounding, such as 0.48 kg and 475 g). 24 changed size between 2026-W40 and 2026-W43, and all 24 kept the same shelf price from 2026-W42 to 2026-W43. 231 had no 2026-W40 pack size and could not be checked, so the true number of size changes could be higher. Where a size fell at the same shelf price within this week’s comparison, the price per unit rose, and the index may not show it (some of the 24 changes may have happened before 2026-W42).

Seven Aldi products are still left out this week. Each changed pack size and gives its unit price in a different measure in the two weeks (such as per 100 g, then per litre), so there is no comparable unit price.

Sensitivity. Under the earlier rule, a missing pack size counted as a change, and 1,278 Aldi products were left out: the 1,263 above, 8 whose pack size was given in 2026-W42 but missing in 2026-W43, and the same 7. With 1,271 of them back in, Aldi’s 2026-W43 level is 99.8622 (99.8128 under the earlier rule), the all-products headline is 99.9645 (99.9522) and food and soft drinks is 100.0195 (100.0210). Leaving out only the 24 products whose size changed gives Aldi 99.8613 and a headline of 99.9643. Almost all of the headline’s week-on-week change comes from Aldi: each retailer has a quarter of the weight, and the other three retailers’ changes together come to about 0.001 points.

Matched products and matched share. matched counts the products found in both weeks with a price, before any are left out as suspect moves or for a pack-size change. A cell needs 20 used products, so it can show 20 or more matched products and still be carried. matchedShare is matched products divided by the cell’s products in the earlier week. It can be above 1 when products move into a class between the weeks because their category changed, as in 2026-W43 for Tesco meat (1.0469) and Sainsbury’s other (1.0121).

Changelog

  • 26 October 2026: new series published, 2026-W42 base rows and the 2026-W43 figures. From this week a pack size missing in one of the two weeks is treated as unknown and the shelf price is compared; in 2026-W43 this brought back 1,271 Aldi products whose pack size was missing in 2026-W42 or 2026-W43.
  • 12 October 2026: week 41 skipped; the index restarted with a new base at 2026-W42 (notice).
  • After 2026-W40: the combined "food and soft drinks" figure, which appeared among the class rows as food-soft-drinks in 2026-W39 and 2026-W40, is published as a subtotal (record types subtotal and retailer_subtotal).
