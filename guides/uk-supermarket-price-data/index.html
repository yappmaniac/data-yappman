Where to get UK supermarket price data: free and paid, compared

What each source measures, how fresh it is, and whether you can download and reuse it, as stated on each publisher's page. Checked on 19 October 2026.

Short answers

Checked against the published pages on 21 October 2026.

What is the Yappman Data UK index?

An index of posted online shelf prices for matched products at Tesco, Sainsbury's, Asda and Aldi, released under CC BY 4.0 and intended to be published weekly, normally on a Monday.

When did it start?

First published on 5 October 2026, with weeks 39 and 40. Week 41 was skipped; the week 41 notice on this site explains how the series continues.

Is it the same as CPI?

No. It is not the consumer prices index and does not show what shoppers paid.

Can I reuse the data?

Yes, under CC BY 4.0. Credit Yappman Data.

How do I cite it?

Use the citation on the index page, which fills in the latest published week: Yappman Data, UK Online Shelf-Price Index, CC BY 4.0, vintage [week], https://data.yappman.com/shelf-price-index/.

Where is the method?

At https://data.yappman.com/shelf-price-index/method/ and https://data.yappman.com/methodology/.

Who do I contact?

info@yappman.com.

If you need UK grocery prices for research, a story or a product, the options split into ONS statistics, research datasets, trackers with no download on their pages, and commercial data. They differ in what they measure (price change or price levels, shelf prices or prices paid), how fresh they are, and whether you can download and reuse them.

ONS statistics

The Office for National Statistics publishes the consumer prices index, including the annual rate for food and non-alcoholic beverages (series D7G8), monthly, with CSV and XLS downloads. Its Shopping prices comparison tool gives average prices for more than 650 items from the CPI basket, from January 2020, with a "download all data" button in the tool. Both are published under the Open Government Licence v3.0.

Best for: the ONS measure of food inflation, monthly. Not for: individual supermarkets or individual products.

Research datasets

University of Warwick, "Price dynamics in food retailing in Great Britain". Weekly prices for 370 products from 1 October 2009 to 31 March 2012, with the data collection method recorded as manual collection from weekly website entries, and the supermarkets anonymised. The UK Data Service record lists it for registered users only (safeguarded data) under the UK Data Service End User Licence; the Warwick repository record describes it as open access under a Creative Commons licence. Check the terms of the copy you use.

LSE (Richard Davies, Josh Hellings and Finn McEvoy). A February 2024 seminar listing describes 19 million online grocery prices collected daily since July 2023 from retailers covering over 70% of UK grocery spending, and a later page by the same authors (Richard Davies, Josh Hellings and Finn McEvoy) describes 25 million prices from seven supermarkets, July 2023 to April 2024. We found no public release of the underlying prices.

Kaggle, “Time Series UK Supermarket Data”. Web-scraped prices from Aldi, Asda, Morrisons, Tesco and Sainsbury's from 9 January 2024, licensed Apache 2.0. Its creator says the scraper is not perfectly reliable, that the data is only available for days on which it was run, that Morrisons data quality was variable until 27 January 2024, and that products show "the lowest price (Nectar, Clubcard etc)" with multi-buy discounts excluded.

Trackers with no download on their pages

Which? supermarket food and drink inflation tracker. Annual inflation at eight supermarkets (Aldi, Asda, Lidl, Morrisons, Ocado, Sainsbury's, Tesco and Waitrose) across 20 categories. Which? says it covers between 23,000 and 28,000 products a month, weighted by supermarket market share and the sales volume of each category, including discounts but not multibuys or loyalty-card offers, and says it moved to once every three months from December 2023. The page offers no data download.

Trolley.co.uk Grocery Price Index. Trolley says its index connects the activity of 850,000+ UK shoppers with its grocery price database, monitoring 47,711 products that are typically purchased at least once a month, and runs from May 2020. The page offers no download or API; it points data enquiries to a contact form.

Our data: Yappman Data

UK Online Shelf-Price Index (free, CC BY 4.0). A weekly index of posted online shelf prices at Tesco, Sainsbury's, Asda and Aldi: the same products matched week on week by the retailer's own product id, a geometric mean (Jevons) of their price ratios with equal weight to each product within a retailer-class cell, equal weight to each retailer and no spending weights, by retailer and by 16 product classes, with matched counts on every row. The weekly snapshots behind it (kept private; only the aggregates are published) hold about 73,000 UK rows a week from the four retailers, one row per product per retailer; that is a snapshot size, not a fixed figure. The index was first published on 5 October 2026, when the base week and the first changing week were published together, and is intended weekly, normally on Monday, when the week's snapshots pass the method's checks. Published index values are not revised except to correct an error, and a missing week restarts the index at 100 with a notice: week 41 was skipped (see the notice). The whole series downloads as one CSV or JSON file. It measures asking prices online, not prices paid, and it is not a CPI. See the method and the data dictionary.

AU Online Shelf-Price Index (free, CC BY 4.0). The same approach for Coles, Woolworths and Aldi Australia, first published on 15 October 2026, normally weekly on Monday (method).

Product-level data. The rows behind the index come from our UK Supermarket Price Scraper on the Apify Store, and are also available as an annual licence; see pricing. A free monthly snapshot of the daily 212-item basket is on Kaggle (a sample, not the full series).

Which to use

  • The ONS rate of food inflation.
  • Supermarket by supermarket, week by week, in a file you can reuse and cite: the Yappman Data index, with its method page to check what it does and does not measure.
  • Historical research on 2009 to 2012: the Warwick data.
  • A weighted headline on supermarket inflation: the Which? tracker (to read; no download on its page).
